Research

Aphasia‑LLM: benchmarking audio language models against clinical aphasia assessment

M.Tech thesis · Prof. Rahul Garg and Prof. N M Anoop Krishnan · M3RG, IIT Delhi

MAY 2026 — PRESENT

Aphasia is an acquired language disorder, most often following stroke. Its severity is graded by trained clinicians administering standardised instruments. This work asks a deliberately narrow question: given the same recordings, how closely do current audio and text language models reproduce those clinical scores?

The benchmark covers four AphasiaBank instruments — a repetition battery, the 15‑item Boston Naming Test short form, and two administrations of a 22‑item verb naming test — over cohorts of roughly 110 to 130 participants. Seven prespecified scoring models are crossed with two ASR systems (Whisper large‑v3 and Deepgram Nova‑3) and five input conditions, from direct audio through to pre‑segmented phonemic transcription.

The design separates evidence from judgement. A transcript is frozen once per participant and reused unchanged across every scoring condition, so ASR effects and scorer effects can be attributed independently rather than confounded. Local code is restricted to work that should be reproducible — contract validation, protocol lookup, aggregation, evidence hashing — and never repairs or reinterprets a model's clinical answer. Reference scores are read at evaluation time only, never during inference. Invalid and missing outputs stay visible in coverage and validity metrics instead of being dropped.

A separate downstream study regresses Western Aphasia Battery measures from the resulting feature matrices under nested cross‑validation, reporting SHAP attribution and bootstrap confidence intervals alongside inference latency and compute telemetry.

The benchmark is a measurement study. It is not a treatment system or a clinical decision tool.
