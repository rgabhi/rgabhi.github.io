Foundations, exactly where they are needed

A prerequisite should help explain an idea rather than become a long detour before the idea. The available chapters contain these self-contained bridges:

  • Vectors, arrays, dot products, and affine neurons.
  • Counting discrete positions and interpreting a floor.
  • Derivatives, the chain rule, and why gradients can shrink.
  • Partial derivatives and accumulation through shared parameters.
  • Expectation and probability-weighted averages through dropout.
  • Mean, variance, standard deviation, and normalization.
  • Chain-rule paths through shared batch statistics.
  • Outcomes, events, and random variables.
  • Expectations, variances, and covariance identities.
  • Covariance matrices, positive definiteness, and dimensions.
  • Likelihoods and the variable being optimized.
  • Trace identities and Gaussian covariance estimation.
  • Log-density ratios, decision boundaries, and posterior probabilities.

The probability chapter now develops those foundations directly; later notes will deepen the algebra, calculus, and statistical modeling connections.

Three ways to use a chapter

Learn it

Read in order. Pause at each checkpoint, draw the diagram yourself, and reproduce the small calculation. If an equation feels mysterious, go back to the meaning and dimensions of each symbol.

Revise it

Attempt the practice problems with solutions closed. Record the reason for a mistake: a missing assumption, a shape error, a forgotten rule, or a concept that is not yet clear. Retry the problem later and use the revision checklist to track what you can explain unaided.

Explain it

Use the interview questions to practice short explanations backed by an example or derivation. Learn the conditions under which a statement is true. “Convolution shares weights” is useful; showing where that sharing appears in both the parameter count and the gradient is stronger.

For interrupted study: the chapter remembers a recently viewed section and your checklist in this browser where local storage is available. The full explanations remain available; quick revision and interview sections are additional paths through the same material.

How these notes are grounded

The lecture slides guide the learning goals and coverage. Explanations add missing steps, prerequisite context, original diagrams, and original practice questions. Supplementary connections are identified where they extend beyond the immediate lecture. Each chapter acknowledges its sources and states where the next topic begins.

The notes are developed one chapter at a time, with time for questions and corrections. If a statement is wrong or a derivation skips the step that matters, that is something to fix in the explanation.
