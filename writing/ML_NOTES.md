# Machine learning notes

The public learning index is `machine-learning.html`, linked from the portfolio's Writing section. The available chapters are `cnn-foundations.html`, `cnn-training.html`, `probability-gaussians.html`, `statistical-regression-gda.html`, `naive-bayes.html`, `factor-models-pca.html`, and `decision-trees-pruning.html`, with links between adjacent chapters and the index.

These are ordinary static pages. GitHub Pages can serve them without a build step. For a local preview, run the existing `serve.py` from the repository root, or use a static HTTP server bound to localhost.

## Extending the collection

1. Inspect all relevant source slides, including annotations. Check the preceding and following lectures before deciding chapter boundaries.
2. Preserve the source learning goals. Explain necessary prerequisites as they arise; expand omitted algebra and supply small worked calculations.
3. Use general topic titles rather than course or exam branding. Attribute sources in each chapter, distinguish supplementary extensions, and avoid copying source photographs or assessment questions.
4. Follow the existing article shell: shared fonts and `article-notes.css`, then the isolated extensions in `ml-notes.css`. Keep existing articles unchanged.
5. Put original diagrams in `assets/ml-notes/`. Give every figure descriptive alternative text, a caption, and a full-size link. Provide static explanations alongside interactive tools.
6. Use `\(...\)` and `\[...\]` delimiters for locally rendered mathematics. State notation, dimensions, conventions, and assumptions. Verify equations and numerical examples.
7. Add practice questions with hints and solutions in distinct disclosure blocks. `data-solution` marks an answer for the chapter's reveal/print controls; `data-hint` marks a hint that remains closed when answers are revealed.
8. Update the learning index when the new chapter is ready. Pending topics remain text, not empty links. Add meaningful links between model chapters and probability, statistics, algebra, and calculus notes as those notes are completed.
9. Verify links, anchors, equation rendering, calculations, mobile layout, and printing. Include a review of keyboard-accessible interactions and any saved reading state.

## Assets and behavior

- `assets/ml-notes.css`: ML-specific layout, diagram, calculator, practice, and print styles.
- `assets/ml-notes.js`: mathematical rendering, answer controls, printing, local reading/checklist state, convolution explorer, and shape calculator.
- `assets/ml-training.js`: dropout masks/scaling and batch-normalization training/inference workbenches, used only by the training chapter.
- `assets/ml-probability.js`: joint-table probabilities and Gaussian covariance/conditioning workbenches, used only by the probability chapter. Charts scroll within their own region on small screens.
- `assets/ml-regression.js` and `assets/ml-regression.css`: regression estimation and Gaussian classification workbenches, plus chapter-specific table/chart layout. The classifier's separate-variance and decision-threshold controls are marked as extensions.
- `assets/ml-naive-bayes.js` and `assets/ml-naive-bayes.css`: categorical count estimation and a document classifier comparing separately fitted multinomial/Bernoulli models. These use a fixed, printed six-document corpus, local tokenization, log scores, and explicit zero-support/unknown-token behavior.
- `assets/ml-pca.js` and `assets/ml-pca.css`: editable two-feature projection geometry, through-origin comparison, and fixed-basis image-patch reconstruction. `assets/ml-pca-patches.js` bundles the original synthetic image, mean, fitted directions, scores and eigenvalues; no library or network service is required for the demos.
- `assets/ml-trees.js` and `assets/ml-trees.css`: editable binary-label threshold search and all five prunings of a fixed tree, with separate training/validation counts, query paths and cost-complexity curves.
- `assets/ml-learning.css` and `assets/ml-learning.js`: opt-in guided learning components. All 22 decision-tree teaching sections use five independent Learn/Revise/Go deeper blocks, explicit lecture-core derivations, running-example diagrams, scaffolded retrieval, a compact revision sheet and a locally saved self-reported review record. The probability chapter uses four independent blocks for sections 1–5 (events through conditioning and Bayes), 6–8 (moments, distribution families and covariance), 9–12 (normalization, joint Gaussianity, geometry and counterexamples), and 13–15 (Gaussian conditioning, workbench reasoning and operations); their records use separate probability-language, probability-moments, probability-gaussian and probability-conditioning keys. The remaining chapters do not yet load these assets.
- `assets/ml-probability-learning.css`: probability-language area and 100-case visuals, compact moment/covariance tables, family navigation, support conditions and variance comparisons. The two original covariance SVG plots under `assets/ml-notes/` show exact linear/nonlinear relationships with explicit population assumptions. Four additional scientific SVGs show scalar standardization, an ellipse before whitening, its circular image afterward, and a non-Gaussian cross with normal marginals. Axis scales preserve the whitening geometry; captions distinguish support from a density. A further four plots compare marginal versus conditional densities, fixed-model observation shifts, and unequal-precision products versus independent random sums. The fourth block includes covariance-block dimensions, a scalar update walkthrough and prediction-first workbench tasks. Static visuals have text explanations and do not require JavaScript.
- `assets/vendor/katex/`: KaTeX 0.19.1 browser files, fonts, and MIT license. They are bundled so equation rendering does not require a third-party network request.
- Shared article fonts and the existing palette/navigation behavior are reused from the portfolio.

Reading state stays in browser local storage where available. It is optional and does not sync to a server. The explanatory HTML remains readable if scripting or storage is unavailable; the interactive calculators require JavaScript.

## Numerical validation

From the repository root, run `python3 tools/verify_ml_training.py`. It uses only the Python standard library. The checks compare analytic convolution/BN derivatives with central finite differences, enumerate dropout masks to verify expectations and variances, and verify worked examples and overlapping pooling. The script deliberately recomputes training BN statistics during perturbations and holds dropout masks fixed when differentiating.

Run `python3 tools/verify_ml_probability.py` for the probability chapter. It also uses only the standard library. Deterministic sums and quadrature verify distribution moments, Gaussian normalization and conditional slices, affine moments, block residual covariance, canonical parameters, ellipse geometry, and products versus convolution of densities. Numerical checks support the derivations; they are not proofs of all possible cases.

Run `python3 tools/verify_ml_regression.py` for regression and GDA. The standard-library checks compare analytic likelihood derivatives with finite differences, independently fit joint/conditional regression, enumerate noise to verify residual degrees of freedom, check pooled versus total covariance, and compare discriminant scores with normalized densities. Singular cases and prior/threshold effects are checked explicitly.

Run `python3 tools/verify_ml_naive_bayes.py` for Naive Bayes. Standard-library enumeration checks sequence and count-vector masses, binary vector normalization, conditional dependence, and sparse affine scores. Exact fractions independently recompute corpus estimates and worked posteriors; finite differences check constrained likelihood derivatives. It also checks pseudocount normalization, zero support, stable log normalization, multiclass probabilities, and duplicated evidence.

Run `python3 tools/verify_ml_pca.py` for factor models and PCA. The standard-library checks independently verify projection identities, constrained variance objectives, the eigenvector/SVD relationship, Gaussian factor moments and latent inference, storage counts, cosine orthogonality, and every direction in the bundled synthetic patch basis. Pixel-space reconstruction is compared with discarded covariance eigenvalues, including zero- and full-component endpoints.

Run `python3 tools/verify_ml_trees.py` for decision trees and pruning. Standard-library enumeration checks impurity concavity, Gini variance identities, threshold partitions, training-error monotonicity, source examples, regression SSE decomposition, and every pruning in the teaching example. Independent exhaustive frontier searches check the bottom-up validation and cost recurrences, including ties and no validation observations. Bootstrap enumeration, covariance averaging and fixed-table significance calculations support the introductory forest/statistics connections.

Run `python3 tools/verify_ml_learning.py` for the complete guided decision-tree chapter. It checks paragraph/equation/anchor retention against published revision `0963f5e` and first-pilot revision `d63c928`, recomputes new examples with exact fractions, and validates HTML, IDs, local links, the five learning blocks and all original exercises. The first pilot’s temporary navigation paragraph is intentionally replaced by the chapter study route. Browser QA separately covers reading modes, storage fallback, keyboard access, responsive layout and print styling.

Run `python3 tools/verify_ml_probability_learning.py` for all four probability learning blocks (sections 1–15). It retains every original paragraph, equation, figure, table and anchor against published baseline `8d75b2b`, preserves the first-block additions from `493a0eb` apart from two forward links, preserves the moments-block additions from `72a74af` apart from its two forward links, preserves the Gaussian-block additions from `344732a` apart from its two forward links, and requires section 16 onward to remain byte-identical. It checks the 100-case visual and static table, independently recomputes moment/covariance examples and matrix feasibility with exact fractions, checks Gaussian interval probabilities, LLᵀ, whitening distances, two-dimensional contour mass, near-singular determinants and the sign-mixture counterexample, checks fresh Gaussian conditionals, observation changes, affine projections, canonical constants, unequal-precision weights and the density-overlap identity, parses the new SVGs, and validates local links and all original exercises. The original workbench markup and shared behavior scripts must remain unchanged unless deliberately extending their functionality. The tree audit leaves intentional probability changes to this separate check; its other chapter guards remain.
